Loop earplugs come with swappable inserts that add extra damping. The inserts are useful when you want them, but easy to misplace when you take them out. I wanted to give them a dedicated place inside the case, without making the case harder to use.

The holder needed to do three things: let an insert go in and out easily, keep it in place while it was not being used, and leave enough clearance for the earplugs and the closed lid.

## Finding a fit

I started by printing single-post test towers in a range of diameters. Trying each one by hand made the tradeoff clear: the insert needed to sit firmly, but still come off without a struggle. That quick fit test was more useful than choosing a dimension on screen and hoping it felt right.

The first holder also had a small cutout for a cord. The idea was to give me something to pull when removing an insert. In practice, the cutout added complexity without solving the main usability problem.

## A simpler orientation

I tried changing the insert's orientation instead. With the loop-shaped insert turned upside down and its wider face exposed, there was enough surface to grip with a fingertip. That made the cord cutout unnecessary, so I dropped it.

The simpler version keeps the inserts turned upside down and adds a place for them on the inside of the case lid. The holder is bonded to the lid, where it stays out of the way while the earplugs are stored.

## The result

The final arrangement keeps the spare inserts together in the lid and leaves the earplugs clear when the case closes. The best change was not adding another feature; it was finding an orientation that made the existing shape easier to use.
